After last year's mascot cookies, for this fall I was inspired to try
making custom vtuber mascot candy molds and candy! I made molds for
[Mint Fantome's](https://www.youtube.com/@mintfantome) mascott Aiboo,
and [Aokumo Rin's](https://www.youtube.com/@aokumorin) mascott Kurimi,
as well as some fudge to test them out! I've got some more ideas and
recipes planned for these molds but I wanted to publish what I have
now since it's Halloween.

## Attempt 1 - Cornstarch

My first attempt at candymaking involved trying to make the mold form
from cornstarch. This is done by some professional confectionaries,
they have big trays that get filled with cornstarch, mold forms are
pressed into the cornstarch to leave impressions, then the candy is
poured into the molds. After the candy hardens, the candies are sifted
out of the cornstarch and the cornstarch is reused for the next batch.

I modeled and 3d printed an aiboo mold form, then filled a small foil
tray to the brim with cornstarch and pressed the aiboo form into it to
leave impressions in the cornstarch.

![Aiboo Form](/images/mascot_mold/cornstarch_mold.jpg){: width="500"}
![Cornstarch Tray](/images/mascot_mold/cornstarch_tray.jpg){: width="500"}

This unfortunately didn't work terribly well. The cornstarch really
liked to stick to the 3d printed form when it was removed from the
tray, especially in small details like the eyes and mouth. This meant
that the cornstarch mold tended to have large defects in those places
which would show up in the final candy. I tried several variations in
technique: packing the cornstarch down, not packing the cornstarch,
sifting the cornstarch, jiggling the mold form as I removed it, and
nothing got me a yield of more than 50% good molds.

I then decided to try filling the molds with jello. I didn't really
expect this to work, but jello was cheap and I had some on hand
already. I however wasn't quite prepared for how bad it would fail:

![Aiboo Form](/images/mascot_mold/cornstarch_filled.jpg)

## Attempt 2 - Silicone

For my second moldmaking attempt, I bought some [food grade silicone resin](https://specialtyresin.com/products/cast-a-mold-platinum-food-grade) to cast a mold in silicone. 

I revised my mold form model to tweak some of the facial features and make the form a bit deeper. I ended up with two facial expressions, a happy and a crying face.

I then printed and heavily sanded these forms to prepare them for casting.

![Aiboo Forms](/images/mascot_mold/silicone_forms_1.jpg)

I also quickly modeled and printed an outer wall to contain the
silicone while it cured. I then hot glued the outer wall and the inner
forms to a sheet of glass, mixed up a portion of the silicone resin
and poured it into the mold.

![Silicone Curing](/images/mascot_mold/silicone_form_curing_1.jpg)

Evidently I didn't do a great job of gluing the form as it leaked
quite a bit. Thankfully I had mixed up enough excess silicone that the
mold still had plenty of silicone in it after leaking. The silicone
released pretty easy from the glass and 3d prints, and after a bit of
cleanup I was left with a shockingly good looking mold.

![Silicone Curing](/images/mascot_mold/silicone_form_released_1.jpg)

## Jello

The first thing I cast in the new molds was some jello, again since I
had some around and it was very easy to make. I ended up needing to
make it about 4x more concentrated than normal jello to get it to
release cleanly and hold its shape, so about 1/2 Cup water to 1 box of
jello. It seemed to work surprisingly well, though the fine details
were quite hard to see on the transparent jello.

![Aiboo Jello](/images/mascot_mold/jello_1.jpg)

## Fudge Again

The second mold worked much better, most of the candies I cast released without cracking. I had some difficulties with voids and blemishes in the surface, and I found that to get a reliable cast:
- do not use cooking spray or oil on the mold, it doesn't help release much and makes loading the mold much harder
- use a silicone spatula or squeeze bottle to load the chocolate, other materials like metal stick too well to the chocolate and make it hard to get it to stay in the mold
- ensure the mold is completely dry before loading, any droplets of water will cause a void in the candy
- make sure the candy is heated sufficiently until it's pretty thin, the times I tried to load the mold with thick fudge didn't work very well
- gently tap the mold on the counter a couple of times after loading to try to dislodge any bubbles on the mold surface.

## Fudge Recipe

- 11 oz can (311 g) sweetened condensed coconut milk
- 2 1/4 cup (420 g) chocolate chips
- pinch of salt
- 1/4 cup (60 ml) bourbon or 1 teaspoon mint extract

Microwave condensed milk and chips until melted. Add bourbon, stir
until mixture is smooth, it will look like you added too much but keep
stirring. Pour into greased 8x8 glass baking dish. Cover and
refrigerate until set, then cut and serve.


For these molds, I made about a half batch of the fudge and cut the
bourbon way down to 1 tsp (2 tsp if making a full batch). From
experience, with 1/4 cup of bourbon in the fudge, it's pretty soft and
I worried that it wouldn't come out of the molds without breaking.
